Evidence and interruption semantics

status.json is written atomically with file and directory fsync before launch, after PID acquisition, and at closure. It records approval, hashes, exact argument arrays, timestamps, attempted/successful counts, process IDs, errors, exit codes and group liveness. A started status without final closure is always incomplete, never passing.

events.jsonl is append-only and fsynced after each command start/end, raw stop PC, breakpoint removal, capture, comparison, failure and capture-finished event. Each row has event and monotonic fields; command rows include stage plus command or output. Comparison rows contain byte count, full SHA-256, post-capture PC, equality and first-difference records (all possible 22 offsets). An interrupted partial trailing line is ignored by the parser but retained verbatim. mainloop.bin is written and fsynced before comparison. xroar.log and gdb.log preserve child stdout/stderr; the JSONL journal is the authoritative durable command transcript because child-native logging may buffer output.

Termination after a completed command preserves its fsynced journal output. Termination during a read cannot manufacture a completed dump; the command_start and partial process logs remain evidence of the missing boundary. SIGKILL or host failure cannot guarantee a final status or cleanup: retain started/partial artifacts and classify the run incomplete. No claim of survival of every hardware/filesystem failure is made.

Deadlines and cleanup procedure

The independent supervisor starts its monotonic clock before XRoar launch. It does not rely on model response timing. Port availability is checked by bind only; readiness uses ss and confirms the listening PID belongs to the new XRoar. No TCP connect/disconnect readiness probe. Four seconds maximum for listener readiness; ten seconds for attach/other GDB operations; fifteen seconds for continue; all observation work stops by second 50. Missing GDB events are still bounded by the outer observation limit.

Each child has its own new process group, and each PID/group is persisted. GDB waits after capture instead of exiting and potentially detaching/resuming the target. On completion, timeout, exception or handled interruption, cleanup sends SIGTERM to the recorded groups, waits at most two seconds within the remaining allowance, escalates to SIGKILL, reaps direct children and checks group liveness. XRoar is terminated before GDB. Native GDB exit can therefore be a termination signal after a successful capture; success uses the fsynced capture-finished marker, actual exit record and dead-group check, not an invented zero exit.

Pass additionally requires total recorded elapsed time at most 60 seconds. A group still alive, unreaped child, missing status, late cleanup or capture failure is failure/incomplete. The supervisor polls deadlines and bounds its subprocess calls; scheduler stalls or uninterruptible OS/filesystem calls can defeat a wall-clock guarantee and must not be described as compliant. Hard-killing the supervisor externally is not a substitute for verified cleanup.
