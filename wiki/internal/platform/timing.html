Timing — frame budget internal

Project-specific decisions on clock rate and interrupt source, plus the Phase 1 frame-budget partition.

Reference content (clock options, IRQ sources, 6809 IRQ/FIRQ timing) is in release/reference/coco3/timing.

Clock rate — locked at 1.78 MHz

Ladybug will run fast — we don't enter BASIC ROM I/O. Decision rationale: doubling the budget halves the cost of every cycle-counting decision and brings Lady Bug's 60-Hz arcade frame within easy reach.

Interrupt source — locked: ACVC Vbord

Decision: Ladybug's main game tick = ACVC Vbord IRQ. That gives us a clean 60 Hz frame interrupt; everything else (animation, AI, input poll, sound dispatch) runs from the same handler. A few µs of jitter to account for: an FIRQ for serial / cartridge can preempt the main IRQ; that's intentional and matches the IRQ < FIRQ < NMI priority order.

Frame partition (first-pass)

29 666 cycles per frame at 1.78 MHz × 60 Hz. Will be revisited by coding-architect:

The 30 Hz off-screen player compositor uses the native compact-stage blitter, and resident gate/respawn/popup redraws obtain the same cache through the seventh bank-3 ABI entry. Fourth optimization completed 2026-07-23: gate_region_to_shadow and gate_region_from_shadow now map PAR5 once at rectangle entry and retain that mapping across scanlines until the 8 KiB window is exhausted. A normal 16-row rectangle eliminates at least 15 repeated mapper calls; after accounting for shadow-pointer stride maintenance, the static lower bound is 138 instructions saved per rectangle, or approximately 1,104 instructions across the eight copies in a four-roaming-enemy movement frame. Scanlines that straddle a page still split at $C000, and PAR5 is restored to physical page $34 on return. Live GDB tests copied and published exact rows across the $3FFF/$4000 boundary. The bank-3 module is 2,845 bytes. The post-series measurement below includes this implementation.

Dirty-union coalescing review 2026-07-23: do not implement runtime coalescing. Only 79 of 1,280 frames had a bounding union smaller than its two 144-byte source rectangles. Next renderer optimization analysis — 2026-07-23

The remaining shadow path is dominated by its per-byte loop rather than mapper setup. Across four 144-byte imports and four publications, the six byte-loop instructions account for 33,408 of 39,640 cycles. The roaming unions are constrained to either 8 bytes by 18 rows or 9 bytes by 16 rows. Add roaming-only word-copy kernels that transfer four LDD/STD pairs per row plus the ninth byte when required. Test once per row for the rare physical-page crossing and retain the existing byte splitter for that row. Keep the generic rectangle copier unchanged for gate and player regions. Static modeling of the captured frame reduces this path to approximately 15,700 cycles, saving about 24,000 cycles or 60%.

The native framebuffer blitter processed 512 cached bytes: 224 were fully transparent, 224 were fully opaque, and 64 contained one transparent nibble. Its current mask-building loop costs 24,064 of the routine's 25,580 cycles. After the word-copy change, specialize this loop with a B-register byte counter, skip zero bytes, store fully opaque bytes directly, and use a destination mask only for the 64 partial bytes. The captured distribution predicts approximately 9,800 additional cycles saved without changing cache layout or using more RAM.

The recommended order is word-copy roaming transfers, re-profile, then sparse native blitting. The two modeled changes together reduce the measured frame from 81,370 to approximately 47,600 cycles. This fits the 59,332-cycle aggregate envelope of the existing 30 Hz movement cadence but not one 29,666-cycle Vbord interval. Meeting a strict single-Vbord limit requires a later ownership change such as persistent ping-pong framebuffers with per-buffer save-under state; that is a renderer protocol refactor, not a local kernel optimization.

Phase-dispatched circular restore — 2026-07-26

The horizontal steady path now tests the packed row phase once per actor and dispatches column phases zero through seven to fixed four-word row loops. Odd phases synthesize the single wrapped word with separate LDA/LDB loads. Any nonzero row phase retains the generic row/column decoder used after vertical movement. This preserves the circular format and consumes 4,010 of the 4,096 bank-3 bytes.

On the controlled four-enemy horizontal transaction, circular restore falls from 4,259 instructions and 20,003 cycles to 923 instructions and 6,131 cycles. The direct like-for-like kernel saving is 13,872 cycles, or 69.3%. The captured complete A-owner interval is 7,358 instructions and 36,801 active cycles; B is 44,078 cycles. These complete-frame totals use a different 30 Hz enemy-mutation cadence from the preceding A capture, so only the restore-path delta is an attributable optimization comparison. Both owners still fail the 29,666-cycle gate.

The fresh live proof again matches 512/512 enemy, 128/128 player, and 512/512 circular save-under bytes with zero writes outside actor footprints. The structural verifier models all eight fixed word orders, repeated horizontal/vertical wraps, and direction changes; the compatibility PERSISTENT_FB=0 image also assembles.

Sparse opaque and partial command lengths now remain in register B instead of being spilled to direct page for every command byte. The framebuffer decoder additionally handles the captured high-frequency opaque lengths four, five, and six with ordered LDD/STD word copies; other lengths retain the generic byte loop. The stage decoder uses the same register counter without framebuffer-only word specialization. Bank-3 use is 4,048 of 4,096 bytes.

On the same controlled four-enemy horizontal transaction, the enemy decode path falls from 13,656 to 12,307 cycles, saving 1,349 cycles or 9.9%. The complete A-owner interval falls from 36,801 to 35,167 active cycles. Its alternating B interval is 45,262 cycles, but the full-owner comparison retains the game's different 30 Hz mutation cadence; the 1,349-cycle decoder delta is the direct attributable result. Both owners still fail the 29,666-cycle gate.

Independent live decoding matches 512/512 enemy, 128/128 player, and 512/512 circular save-under bytes with no out-of-footprint writes. The framebuffer hash is d3f45f12a5424c88763a5b7a86b5e38982da6ccb937a55cb6cac3ea5993e32bd. Structural verification models the specialized byte order for all three lengths, and the PERSISTENT_FB=0 image assembles.

Strict-60-Hz architecture designed 2026-07-23: the persistent ping-pong framebuffer removes normal shadow import/publication by rendering directly into the non-scanned physical framebuffer and selecting it with one $FF9D write at bottom-border Vbord. Per-buffer actor history and damage ledgers keep the older snapshot convergent. Precompiled sparse enemy/player streams and strip-updated circular save-under state provide the additional reduction required beyond the 39,640-cycle copy removal. The modeled steady four-enemy range is 24,500-27,500 cycles; implementation must prove every steady path below 29,666 cycles. In the accepted overload protocol, raw Vbord continues counting but simulation advances once per committed image. A missed Vbord freezes the current logical/render epoch until it commits instead of skipping transient states or catching up timers.
