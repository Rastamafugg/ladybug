Ticket Workflow internal

From conversation intake through approval, delegation, integration, and closure.

Principles

  • The ticket page is the canonical work contract; conversation explains and approves it.
  • The roadmap defines milestones; the ticket index defines executable order.
  • Approval fixes outcome, scope, targets, and acceptance criteria. Assignment adds execution detail without changing them.
  • Measured, projected, historical, and required values must be labeled separately and tied to a source revision or artifact.
  • Implementation completion is not acceptance. Failed targets remain failed and produce a linked follow-up or a return to Ready.

Lifecycle

  1. Draft: intake exists, but requirements or evidence are incomplete.
  2. Proposed: approval brief is complete and awaits explicit user approval.
  3. Approved: the user approved outcome, scope, targets, and acceptance criteria.
  4. Ready: dependencies, design, owned artifacts, commands, evidence, and commit boundaries are complete.
  5. In Progress: one owner is executing the assignment contract.
  6. Verification: implementation is complete and the QA role is evaluating current-revision evidence.
  7. Done: all required criteria pass, evidence and commits are recorded, and integration is complete.

Blocked records a concrete external dependency or repeated impasse; Deferred preserves valid work outside the current plan; Rejected records a declined proposal and rationale; and Superseded links to the replacement ticket. Side states must not erase evidence or decision history.

Approval brief

The approval response and proposed ticket must contain:

  • direct recommendation and intended outcome;
  • why the work matters now and how it fits the roadmap;
  • observed baseline with source revision and evidence artifact;
  • in-scope and out-of-scope boundaries;
  • normal scenarios, rare paths, edge cases, replay/integration cases, and failure modes;
  • options, tradeoffs, rejected alternatives, dependencies, ordering, risks, and capacity implications;
  • target improvement and exact success measurements;
  • what user decision advances the ticket to Approved.

Before the first response table, define labels and terminology, units, baseline, target, margin formula, scenario or owner names, aggregation method, evidence freshness, and pass/fail thresholds. For example, state whether a cycle figure is a complete-frame maximum or a projected subroutine saving before displaying either value in a table.

Assignment and subagent contract

Assignment begins only from a Ready ticket. The assignment repeats the approved objective and acceptance criteria, then adds exact files, ownership boundaries, worktree or branch, constraints, required commands, retained evidence, wiki updates, separate commit requirement, and completion report format. It must explicitly prohibit scope drift.

  • Give one subagent one bounded ticket or independently committable step.
  • Do not parallelize unresolved dependencies or shared writable files without a named integration owner and conflict plan.
  • Require periodic reports of current measurement versus target, not only activity summaries.
  • Require rejected experiments and regressions to be reported and reverted before commit.
  • Require a separate commit with tests, evidence, capacity, residual risk, and target result.
  • Do not let subagents push or close tickets unless the assignment explicitly grants that authority.

Integration and verification

  • Inspect and integrate each delegated commit separately.
  • Regenerate maps, binaries, traces, and reports from combined source; never resolve generated artifacts by selecting a stale side.
  • Recheck combined memory, payload, stack, direct-page, and other hard capacity limits.
  • Force required rare paths. Missing required scenarios fail verification.
  • Test natural replay, alternating ownership, cadence coupling, and other sequences that isolated forcing can conceal.
  • Report hardware, engineering, behavioral, diagnostic, and capacity targets independently.
  • Keep temporary captures untracked unless the ticket names them as retained evidence.

Conversation response forms

Approval response

  1. State the recommendation and decision required.
  2. Define terminology, units, scenarios, baselines, targets, margins, and evidence before any table.
  3. Summarize current evidence and why work is needed.
  4. List each proposed item with work, intended improvement, success measurement, dependencies/order, risks, and exclusions.
  5. Explain alternatives and identify what approval authorizes.

Assignment message

  1. Name and link the Ready ticket; repeat the approved objective and non-goals.
  2. Assign exact artifacts, ownership boundary, worktree/branch, and dependency position.
  3. Specify implementation constraints, required scenarios, targets, commands, evidence, and wiki updates.
  4. Require one bounded commit and a completion report containing result versus target, tests, capacity, rejected experiments, and residual risk.

Progress and completion response

  1. Report current measured result versus target, completed evidence, remaining work, and concrete blockers.
  2. At completion, distinguish implementation success from acceptance, state the justified lifecycle transition, and identify any follow-up ticket.

Ticket maintenance

Every lifecycle transition adds a dated decision-log entry. Update the ticket index status, priority, order, and dependencies in the same change. Completion records commit hashes, commands, evidence links, measured results, rejected experiments, residual risks, capacity changes, and the QA verdict. Append a corresponding entry to the project log.

Any material change to an approved outcome, scope, target, acceptance criterion, dependency order, or risk profile returns the ticket to Proposed for renewed approval. Completed, rejected, deferred, and superseded tickets remain indexed as history; they are never deleted merely to keep the active queue short.

This workflow is enforced by AGENTS.md and the role skills under .agents/skills/.
