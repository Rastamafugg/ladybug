Approval brief

Objective and motivation

The instruction screen must visibly teach the three bonus systems in descending screen order: EXTRA, then SPECIAL, then the three hearts followed by the skull. The sequence replaces the current six fixed 32-frame phases with colour-clocked, row-local progression and the authored terminal rewards.

Observed versus expected

Observed at 135b965: scripts/build_presentation.py flattens every visible tile layer except Sprite Animations. It therefore treats Sprite Locations as static screen content and fails on its transformed Lady Bug tile. The pre-update runtime uses six hard-coded destinations and a 192-frame total schedule; it does not consume EXTRA or SPECIAL letter by letter, update the displayed point value, publish the authored rewards, run the full skull-death sequence, or hold the final angel.

Expected: one global red/yellow/blue collectible cycle runs throughout the instruction sequence with a nominal dwell of roughly one second per colour. The exact frame counts come from the required oracle. Every colour transition updates the displayed collectible value: red shows 800 POINTS, yellow shows 300 POINTS, and blue shows 100 POINTS. A row consumes at most one target on each transition into its trigger colour.

Scope

In: arcade capture, exact colour and pause timing, semantic TMX layer parsing, three sequential row states, animated Lady Bug movement and underlay restoration, EXTRA/SPECIAL letter consumption and HUD lighting, heart multipliers, point-value replacement, reward surfaces, skull collision, the complete Lady Bug death sequence, held angel ending, terminal pause, global credit/start pre-emption, generated choreography records, worklist timing, and capacity. Out: live-game bonus/scoring rules, new renderers, sound, title actors, credit-screen behavior, demo gameplay, and later high-score/name flow.

Timing terminology and pass rule

A colour dwell is the number of committed 60 Hz Vbord presentation epochs for one red, yellow, or blue state. The user requirement is approximately one second; the oracle supplies the exact required dwell and may vary only if the arcade capture proves it. A terminal pause is the committed interval from the first complete held white-angel frame to the request for the next attract screen; the user requirement is a few seconds and the oracle supplies its exact target. Forced-state evidence does not replace the complete natural sequence. Missing oracle frames, rows, reward states, death frames, or pre-emption paths fail verification.

Golden path

  1. Initialize: publish the static instruction screen without Sprite Locations, set the collectible cycle to its oracle-backed starting colour/value, and draw animated Lady Bug at the EXTRA start from the metadata layer.
  2. EXTRA row: run the shared red → yellow → blue cycle. On each transition into yellow, latch exactly one ordered target from E, X, T, R, A; animate Lady Bug until it overlaps and consumes that letter; then turn the corresponding left-HUD EXTRA character yellow. Other colour transitions change the value display but do not consume an EXTRA letter.
  3. EXTRA completion: after A is consumed, publish the authored 2×2 Lady Bug life-counter reward at the right of the top row and place animated Lady Bug at the SPECIAL start. Preserve the completed yellow HUD state.
  4. SPECIAL row: continue the same colour clock without a row-local reset. On each transition into red, consume one ordered target from S, P, E, C, I, A, L and turn the corresponding left-HUD SPECIAL character red.
  5. SPECIAL completion: after L is consumed, publish the authored 2×2 coin reward at the right of the second row and place animated Lady Bug at the heart-row start. Preserve both completed HUD entries.
  6. Heart row: on each transition into blue, consume exactly one heart. The first, second, and third pickups light the left-HUD ×2, ×3, and ×5 entries respectively and update both right-side point-row multiplier fields to X2, X3, and X5 respectively.
  7. Skull and death: after the third heart and X5 publication, continue Lady Bug to the skull at the right side of the row and consume it as the terminal collision. The skull remains outside bonus-colour recolouring. Play the complete Lady Bug death animation in order, but replace the ordinary flying-angel continuation with the authored white angel-wings endpoint and hold that surface in place.
  8. Handoff: retain the final completed HUD/rewards, point-row X5, and white angel for the oracle-backed few-second pause. Then request the next screen in the unattended attract sequence, currently level start, through the existing atomic screen handoff.

Scenarios and edge cases

  • The colour scheduler is global across row boundaries; completing a row does not create an extra red/yellow/blue transition or double-trigger the next row.
  • A trigger is edge-latched. A long dwell, delayed publication, or multi-frame Lady Bug movement cannot consume a second target during the same colour state.
  • Point-value replacement occurs on every colour transition, including transitions while a row is complete or Lady Bug is moving. Old digits must be fully restored before new digits publish.
  • Completed letters, HUD highlights, row rewards, and multiplier fields persist across both framebuffer owners and later row activity.
  • Heart multipliers update both point rows in the same committed epoch as the corresponding HUD highlight; partial X2/X3/X5 publication fails.
  • The skull is static and is not recoloured by the bonus cycle. No angel frame appears before the ordered death frames complete.
  • A credit edge in any row, movement, death, or pause state pre-empts before the next mutation and opens the credit screen. A valid Player 1 edge with credit pre-empts before the next mutation and selects level start. Held keys do not repeat; simultaneous input follows the established global edge priority.
  • Cold and warm presentation entry reset row, target, colour-event latch, movement, death, reward, and underlay state. Stale RAM cannot skip a target or start from a later row.
  • A missing, renamed, duplicate, overlapping, clipped, transformed unexpectedly, or structurally incomplete metadata record fails generation rather than being flattened or ignored.
