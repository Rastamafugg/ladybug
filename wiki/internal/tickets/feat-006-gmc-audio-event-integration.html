Revised approval brief

Objective and motivation

Produce one complete-profile 65,536-byte GMC cartridge containing all current attract, instruction, demo, gameplay, game-over, name-entry, and high-score presentation data plus the approved production audio payload. Recover enough cartridge source capacity to eliminate the measured 3,813-byte deficit and retain at least 2,048 CPU-readable source bytes after all required streams, descriptors, decompression code, and phase-swap data are included.

The current complete profile omits required presentation or audio content depending on profile selection. The revised design removes that profile split without adding gameplay-time cartridge bank switching or changing the visible presentation, cue notes, cue timing, score behavior, or existing framebuffer protocol.

Measured evidence and baseline

All byte counts in this section are single current-worktree measurements or conservative projections from base revision 18e66aafb765c2a76719c53ea3100ebb0ad0f5b4 plus the uncommitted FEAT-006 implementation in the assigned worktree. The effective CPU-readable copied-payload source pool is 41,438 bytes. The complete presentation, current 4,830-byte audio payload, 331-byte helper, and 2-byte proof require 45,251 source bytes, producing the exact baseline deficit of 3,813 bytes.

  • Unified presentation: the full six-map union is 15,073 raw bytes. Two destination-page-bounded LZSS streams are 2,115 and 3,107 bytes, or 5,222 total. Replacing the current complete-profile 9,643-byte presentation payload adds all high-score content while saving 4,421 source bytes.
  • High-score auxiliary: the 907-byte phase runtime compresses to 833 bytes, a saving of only 74. The capacity model therefore retains the full raw 907 bytes.
  • Audio: the current 4,830-byte payload compresses to 2,429 bytes, saving 2,401.
  • Physical page $39: the combined player, gate, and presentation sparse source is 4,022 bytes and compresses to 1,164 bytes, saving 2,858.
  • Decoder: a generic bounded 6809 LZSS core assembles to 63 bytes. Replacing the existing presentation-only decoder and adding stream setup is projected to grow bootstrap code by 70–120 bytes. The projected free bootstrap gap is not counted as capacity savings.
  • Round trips: host-side expansion reproduced the audio and page-$39 inputs byte for byte. The measured expanded SHA-256 prefixes were 14fea73f336a7a0d for audio and deb032745f4c883c for page $39. These are design evidence; implementation acceptance requires fresh full hashes and byte comparisons.

The conservative required-source projection is 45,251 - 4,421 - 2,401 - 2,858 + 907 = 36,478 bytes. Against the 41,438-byte source pool this leaves 4,960 bytes. Reserving approximately 128 bytes for two phase-overlaid tile patch sets gives an expected margin of approximately 4,832 bytes, or 4.8 KiB. This projection exceeds the 2 KiB acceptance target by approximately 2.8 KiB.

Approved outcome requested

  1. Unified presentation compression: compile the complete six-map presentation union, including attract, instructions, level start/demo, gameplay-supporting surfaces, game over, enter-high-score, and high-score presentation, into two independently bounded LZSS streams that expand only into physical pages $3A and $3B.
  2. Audio compression: LZSS-compress the complete approved cue descriptor and command-stream payload, then expand it once during launch into physical page $3D. Runtime playback reads RAM and performs no cartridge bank switching or frame-time decompression.
  3. Page-$39 compression: combine and compress the player, gate, and presentation sparse source for launch-time expansion into physical page $39, retaining existing indexes and runtime sparse decoding.
  4. Phase-overlaid tile slots: retain the one-byte tile-ID protocol. Use two tile slots whose 32-byte graphics are replaced only at instruction/high-score phase boundaries. Stage both 64-byte patch sets on page $23; do not introduce a 9-bit map format.
  5. Phase-owned $0300 runtime: keep the existing mutually exclusive auxiliary destination. Attract/instruction phases install the instruction or demo auxiliary; gameplay installs the full audio runtime; game-over/name entry explicitly completes or stops the death cue, mutes the PSG, then installs the high-score auxiliary. The high-score auxiliary owns a compact timer-tick player for cue 4. Leaving high score restores the presentation auxiliary, and starting gameplay reinstalls the full audio runtime.

Scope

In scope: one shared launch-time LZSS decoder; build-time packing and exact decompression proofs; descriptor-driven expansion into pages $39, $3A, $3B, and $3D; page-$23 staging; two tile-slot swaps; explicit $0300 installer and ownership transitions; the high-score timer tick; complete-profile build integration; loader identity verification; natural end-to-end execution; and synchronized source, generated artifacts, tests, ticket, memory-map, and log records.

Out of scope: enemy sparse/runtime compression, resident bank-1 compression or ABI relocation, perimeter-reset precomputation, a 9-bit tile-map protocol, gameplay-time decompression, gameplay-time cartridge reads, a full audio mixer during name entry, new cues, cue retuning, presentation redesign, physical-cartridge validation, and unrelated profile cleanup. Any need for these items returns the ticket to Proposed.

Runtime ownership and failure behavior

  • Each compressed descriptor identifies source bank/address, compressed end, destination physical page/address/end, compressed hash, and expanded hash. The loader rejects build-time interval overlap and the verifier rejects length or identity mismatch.
  • Every stream is bounded by its declared destination page. No expansion may cross an 8 KiB physical-page boundary or overwrite the loader, stack, framebuffer state, game state, resident code, or another expanded payload.
  • The $0300-$0697 region has one owner at a time. Every transition mutes or quiesces the outgoing audio owner before overwrite and installs the incoming runtime completely before dispatch.
  • Warm reset, cold reset, startup, and phase replacement leave all SN76489 voices muted until a cue is explicitly requested. The FEAT-004 mute-write and PIA route behavior remains mandatory.
  • A build-time packing, capacity, source/destination overlap, round-trip, or hash failure stops the build. There is no fallback that silently drops a map, cue, or verification gate.

Scenarios and distinct risks

  • Exact host round trips: covers compressor/decompressor format errors and truncated or overrun streams for every required packed artifact.
  • Complete-profile capacity build: covers the 64 KiB image boundary, CPU-readable source-pool deficit, destination-page bounds, generated interval collisions, and minimum 2,048-byte source margin.
  • Loader identity: compares authored bytes, staged compressed bytes, and expanded live destination bytes before interpreting runtime behavior; covers source-bank selection, descriptor bounds, MMU destination mapping, and decompressor correctness.
  • Natural attract-to-gameplay-to-high-score execution: covers presentation tile swaps and $0300 ownership in their real order: cold attract, instructions, level start/demo, credit and live gameplay, player death/game over, qualifying name entry with timer tick, END or timeout, updated high-score display, return to attract, and subsequent gameplay reinstall.
  • Reset and replacement: covers startup, warm reset, cold reset, gameplay-to-name-entry audio shutdown, SPACE-equivalent explicit stop where applicable, and absence of stuck tones after runtime replacement.

No Cartesian combination of every framebuffer owner, presentation phase, and cue is required. Existing focused owner tests remain valid; the natural sequence is required because phase order and runtime replacement are the new independent risks.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every required compressed presentation, audio, and page-$39 stream expands byte-for-byte to its authored input. Fresh full SHA-256 identities, byte counts, and destination bounds are retained.
  • LADYBUG_PROFILE=complete bash scripts/build.sh build produces an exact 65,536-byte GMC image containing every required map and approved cue, with no omitted-profile placeholder and no interval or capacity failure.
  • The final generated capacity report shows at least 2,048 CPU-readable copied-payload source bytes free. The expected result is approximately 4,832 bytes; a result below 2,048 fails even if the image builds.
  • Loader verification proves authored, staged, and expanded destination identity for pages $39, $3A, $3B, and $3D, and proves that all descriptors and destinations are disjoint and page bounded.
  • The natural complete-profile sequence reaches attract, instructions, demo/gameplay, game over, qualifying name entry, updated high score, return to attract, and a later gameplay start without corruption, stale tile variants, invalid dispatch, or stalled ownership handoff.
  • Gameplay cues use the full approved audio runtime; name entry emits the timer tick from its compact phase-owned path; game-over/name-entry replacement and startup/warm/cold reset leave no stuck or startup tone.
  • Existing complete-profile visual, score, framebuffer, sparse-render, audio cue, reset-mute, and loader checks pass without weakening their assertions.

Options and recommendation

Recommended: implement the five-part bounded design above. It directly addresses the measured deficit, keeps all decompression at launch, preserves one-byte tile IDs, and establishes explicit phase ownership. The expected 4.8 KiB margin leaves approximately 2.8 KiB beyond the acceptance floor without relying on speculative recovery.

Rejected for this revision: a 9-bit tile map increases map and runtime complexity to solve only two tile IDs; keeping the full mixer during name entry conflicts with the high-score auxiliary at $0300; precomputed perimeter reset consumes a projected net 2,070 additional ROM bytes; compressing tiny presentation/demo code gives negligible or negative savings; and recompressing the existing attract bundle grows it. Enemy and resident compression remain reserve options only if a measured implementation regression consumes the planned margin.

Dependencies, order, and stop conditions

  1. Freeze and hash each raw input; implement and prove the shared stream format and exact host round trips.
  2. Integrate launch expansion and descriptor bounds one destination page at a time, proving authored/staged/destination identity before runtime diagnosis.
  3. Integrate tile-slot swaps and $0300 phase ownership, then run focused reset/replacement checks.
  4. Run the complete capacity build and loader identity suite, then the single natural lifecycle sequence.

Stop before exceeding the approved total 25% ceiling, if projected or measured source margin falls below 2,048 bytes, if any stream cannot remain destination-page bounded, if the shared decoder requires a runtime ABI or MMU change beyond this brief, if the name-entry timer needs the full mixer, or if a required existing behavior must be removed. Report completed evidence, remaining work, revised estimate, and lower-cost alternatives before requesting renewed approval.

Implementation constraints

  • The host codec and 6809 decoder implement one documented bounded LZSS stream. Every descriptor contains source bank/start/end, destination physical page/start/end, raw length, compressed length, and full compressed/raw SHA-256 identities in the generated manifest.
  • Presentation expands only to pages $3A/$3B, audio only to page $3D, and the combined player/gate/presentation sparse payload only to page $39. A stream may not cross an 8 KiB destination-page boundary.
  • All expansion occurs before resident handoff. The bootstrap verifies descriptor termination, restores PAR5 to page $34, enters all-RAM mode, and preserves existing authored/staged/destination identity rules.
  • Page $23 stages the instruction, demo, and high-score auxiliaries plus exactly two 64-byte tile patch sets. The presentation runtime copies only the selected 32-byte tile variants at instruction/high-score phase boundaries.
  • The $0300-$0697 destination has exactly one installed owner. Gameplay-to-game-over/name-entry waits for cue completion or explicitly stops and mutes before installing the high-score auxiliary. The high-score auxiliary implements only cue 4 timer tick. Return-to-attract and later gameplay reinstall the correct presentation or full audio owner before dispatch.
  • Startup, warm reset, cold reset, and every owner replacement retain the four-write SN76489 mute sequence and valid PIA audio route. No tone may begin without an explicit cue request.
  • The existing 4,832-byte margin is a projection, not permission to add unrelated data. The implementation passes only at >=2,048 source bytes free.
