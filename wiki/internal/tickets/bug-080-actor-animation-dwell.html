BUG-080 — Arcade actor animation dwell Draft

High-risk · P2 · created 2026-10-01 · baseline fe160ae389145d24083218ded05ba9cdca781474 plus nine unchanged dirty TMX inputs

Approval brief

Objective and motivation

Match Lady Bug and all enemy sprite animation poses to measured arcade dwell. Keep pose dwell separate from travel speed and avoid a blanket scheduler slowdown.

Observed behavior and evidence

The user reports that loop ordering matches but actor poses appear to dwell too briefly. Historical BUG-050 measured an eight-MAME-frame player pose hold and only a partial enemy sample; it is not full coverage. Current source reloads PLAYER_ANIM_TIMER and ENEMY_TIMER at eight scheduled ticks. That is a source fact, not proof that either timer mismatches the arcade. Current-attract measurements show four decorative sprites at eight MAME frames per pose, not gameplay actors. Raw current-run identity and counts are in RSCH-014-B evidence.

Animation-family audit

  • Player walking: historical BUG-050 covers a limited run; current per-state, direction-reset, and gameplay cadence coverage is incomplete.
  • Roaming enemies: the source documents eight initial type groups and adjacent-group selection after part eight. Forced MAME captures match sprite groups 11, 17, 29, 23, 35, 41, 47, and 53, but do not establish the dwell for every pose or direction.
  • Dormant den and release: enemy release reference distinguishes the four-frame den actor from released actors; it does not measure every independent pose interval.
  • Death, wings, and angel: death animation reference already records exact special-sequence intervals. Preserve those timings unless a new discrepancy is measured; do not convert them to the roaming timer.
  • Instruction and attract actors: instruction reference and attract actor reference contain phase-specific behavior. The current attract ROI is decorative evidence only.
  • Logo phases are separate in BUG-081. Collectible score popups already have a separate measured hold contract and are not actor locomotion.

Cause confidence and discrimination

Cause remains unconfirmed. A shared eight-tick CoCo timer may be too short, or the mismatch may be scheduled-tick cadence, sprite-state selection, or a different arcade frame-to-CoCo tick mapping. Current arcade evidence now contains limited exact Part-1 player and roaming-enemy pose intervals, but the CoCo clock conversion and complete actor-family coverage are still open. Measure contiguous pixel-state runs for each included family before changing a timer. Use distinct samples for independent clock owners; do not use a type-by-direction Cartesian matrix unless source evidence shows independent direction clocks. Record displacement separately from pose transitions. The source-owner audit is linked below.

Scope and acceptance

In: measured player and enemy pose dwell across the family inventory above, including any distinct roaming, den, release, or gameplay actor clock that source inspection confirms. Out: movement velocity, collision and AI rules, logo timing, popup display duration, broad scheduler slowdown, and already measured death art unless a conflicting measurement is obtained.

Acceptance requires a current arcade ROM/run identity, exact pose-state intervals, complete included-family coverage, preserved special-sequence references, and unchanged tile displacement at the accepted movement cadence. Missing required family or ambiguous state transitions fail evidence. CoCo cadence mapping must be measured, not inferred from MAME frame counts.

Class, recommendation, and dependencies

High-risk: actor cadence shares scheduled gameplay work with movement and collision, timing is a hard behavior boundary, and renderer state is persistent. First obtain measured arcade family coverage; then compare a current-snapshot CoCo build and prove timing separation. The current C3 complete-build assignment is available. The remaining CoCo timing gate is to map gameplay scheduled ticks to raw Vbords and published-pose intervals; this is distinct from the established one-MAME-frame-to-one-CoCo-Vbord unit conversion. Reclassify if the cause crosses into adaptive scheduling, memory, or framebuffer ownership.

Decision

Draft. Current Part-1 player and roaming-enemy samples narrow the arcade target, but complete family coverage and CoCo-to-MAME clock mapping remain missing. No approval is requested.
