Confirmed route behavior and limits

The recorded input-only route is deterministic but not goal-directed. It retains direction index 3 from the first contact across the death and replacement join. That index is East in SEARCH_SOURCE_DIRECTIONS=(North, South, West, East). The route travels east from (12,18) to (22,18), north to (22,13), and encounters the skull at (22,12) as its next north cell. The skull guard correctly releases before that cell. The fixed direction-index rotation then selects South, runs to the bottom edge, selects West, runs across row 22, selects East to return, and then selects North back to (22,13). The same skull boundary sends it south again. This repeats; at timeout the controller has rotated back to West at (13,22). It never takes a lateral bypass around the skull toward the last sampled enemy at (22,10).

This is a confirmed limitation of InputOnlySearch's fixed local direction rotation: at a blocked prospective cell it advances to the next index, not the next route edge toward a target. At the north skull boundary that means reversing South. It records no enemy goal, no visited-path cycle, and no alternate-route choice. The player did make substantial legal progress, so a three-cell stall is not the explanation.

The skull exclusion is required. In src/main.s, player_tick calls check_entity_pickup at each arrived cell; an exact ENTITY_SKULL match enters cep_skull and starts player death. Separately, src/enemy_runtime.s enemy_skull_test removes an enemy when its cell matches a skull. Enemy contact is checked before enemy movement and accepts a cell difference of at most one in both axes. Consequently the desired path goal can be any safe reachable cell in that Chebyshev-one contact neighborhood, but the path must not enter a live skull cell. The final enemy-table sample and the release marker are not enough to prove that the same enemy traveled from (12,10) to (22,10), or that its position was reachable at every prior player waypoint. The release and active counters are consistent with a later release and an enemy removal, but this trace does not timestamp or identify that removal.

Source cadence is not a timing diagnosis. src/main.s documents gameplay player movement at 30 Hz (two pixels every second VBlank); enemy_tick runs on the gameplay VBlank path. src/enemy_runtime.s decrements ENEMY_TIMER for nest animation and separately calls the $A3C5 rate helper for fractional enemy movement; it checks enemy-player overlap before movement and checks enemy skulls after a cell arrival. The rate helper also observes freeze and pickup gates. The final sample has FREEZE_TIMER 0, but there is no continuous enemy-position history. Stage-one perimeter release reloads BOX_TIMER to 9 and invokes enemy_release after the 92-box circuit; the trace has already observed the required second-life ordinary-release marker. These facts do not establish an emulator or production movement slowdown.
