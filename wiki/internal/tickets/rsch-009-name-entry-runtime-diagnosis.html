Bounded probes and distinct risks

There are at most three distinct runtime experiments below. Each has a 60-second wall-clock deadline for the whole phase, including tool waits; it is not a renewable timeout per tick. Permit at most one deterministic rerun after an identified probe-mechanics correction, also limited to 60 seconds. Thus the maximum aggregate runtime-probe wall time is 240 seconds, excluding build, source analysis and report writing. Parent confirmation replay must use the remaining allowance. Never extend a deadline automatically.

  1. Identity and first publication. Risk: stale build, wrong mapped helper or an artificial fixture masquerading as the delivered flow. Cold-start the built complete GMC ROM. Establish mainloop and loaded module identity; record keyboard credit/start and progression markers. If playing to final death would exceed the phase, seed only the required game-over state in that cold-started session and label the transition forced. Let actual game-over timer and name hydration/publication run. Success marker: mode NAME, enter-high-score screen, completed hydration and a published cursor frame. Compare authored/build payload, staged bytes and live installed bytes for the low-RAM name runtime, page-$23 helper, player payload and relevant cold map/edge/timer data before interpretation. Record mappings and restore any temporary observation aliases. Timeout means boot, identity or transition was not established; it is not evidence of slow game code. Do not continue to later probes if identity is unproven.
  2. Sprite movement, idle and timer publication. Risk: logical jumping versus retained pixels or timer-coupled publication. From the established name state, use a short open route: move two adjacent tile distances, release mid-traversal, idle, resume/reverse, and cross the first timer update without changing its source code. Record state at move, restore, capture, draw and IRQ publication boundaries. Success marker: captured successive published frames plus both owners' cursor pointers and save-under bytes, sufficient to locate the first divergence or show no reproduction. Compare each selected owner's old rectangle with its own pre-cursor background; use the real sprite mask rather than treating all 16×16 pixels as opaque. Include one isolated restore/draw replay into each owner from equivalent clean state. If blaming owner identity, repeat with starting owner reversed and identical pixels, metadata and inputs. Timeout means the requested sequence or publication was not captured. Missing natural sequence or isolated-owner evidence stays incomplete.
  3. Collision boundary and open-corridor control. Risk: incorrect topology, sprite-footprint overlap, or a legal turn delayed until alignment. Choose one reachable internal-wall endpoint and adjacent authored opening from the TMX; record exact screen/local coordinates before executing. Approach the wall and opening from the two axes needed to exercise above/right sprite extent; include a mid-step perpendicular input and a reversal. Add one perimeter approach as the distinct footprint control. Success marker: each attempt reaches name_joy_ready and its next completed publication with direction, PLAYER_WANT, anchor, remaining steps, edge mask and opaque sprite pixels captured. Compare gameplay policy from source; do not copy its dynamic gate side effects into the oracle. Use an unforced connected route from name entry for the normal case, with isolated forced approach only for the otherwise-unreached edge case. Expected legality must come from authored wall/corridor geometry and user requirements, not the generated mask alone. If intent is ambiguous, classify it as an unresolved behavior question. Timeout means the approach/result was not observed, not that a wall blocked it correctly.

Global stop: two rejected experiments, two observation-tool timeouts, a material expansion into another subsystem, inability to establish artifact identity, exhausted experiment allowance, or forecast cost above the ceiling. Report completed evidence, remaining candidates, reason for the stop, revised estimate and lower-cost option before seeking further authorization. One timeout requires inspecting the last marker and observation mechanics before another attempt. Required paths cannot be omitted and marked passing.
