Implementation · architecture

Persistent Ping-Pong Framebuffer internal

Phased replacement for region import/publication, targeting one complete steady-gameplay transaction per 60 Hz Vbord interval.

Status and decision

Implemented persistent A/B framebuffer protocol. Physical pages $30-$33 and $2C-$2F become equal persistent framebuffer A/B snapshots. The GIME scans one while PAR1 through PAR4 map the other for rendering. A completed back buffer is committed at Vbord by changing only $FF9D; normal gameplay no longer imports or publishes dirty rectangles.

Phase 2 now enforces the first ownership boundary: gameplay routines mutate authoritative state and publish compact render intents, while bank-3 entry $0818 is the single per-Vbord framebuffer owner. Enemy logic snapshots prior actor pointers and save-under ownership before mutation; bank-3 entry $0815 consumes those snapshots during rendering. Gate intents retain up to two ordered operations so a pending final frame and a new diagonal frame cannot overwrite each other in one epoch. A build-time structural proof rejects direct framebuffer calls from player, enemy, gate, score, pickup, death, and timer mutation ranges.

The persistent A/B protocol is implemented. Game logic does not draw directly, each framebuffer owns its actor restoration state, and background mutations remain pending until both buffers have projected the current logical state.

GIME scanout uses the physical vertical-offset address independently of the CPU MMU. Both buffers are 8 KiB aligned, so their low offset byte is always zero and the swap is one write to $FF9D. The documented Vbord IRQ occurs when vertical scan reaches the bottom border, leaving the lower border and vertical blank before the next active image. The IRQ handler is therefore the sole display-base owner. Real-hardware Phase 10 must still verify that the write is latched without a split frame.

Interpreting A/B performance labels

An A- or B-owner measurement identifies the physical framebuffer used as BACK for that interval; it does not identify a fixed class of work or imply that one buffer is intrinsically slower. Successive owners can receive different logical phases, pending-damage replay, actor history, ring phases, and capture modes. Performance evidence must therefore name the scenario phase and executed worklist first, with framebuffer owner as secondary metadata. An owner-specific timing claim requires a controlled crossover: identical starting pixels, ownership metadata, and render intents rendered once into each buffer, with the starting order reversed.

Every field read or written by the IRQ, including the FRONT/BACK IDs, readiness flag, sequence counters, and diagnostics, must live in always-mapped physical page $38. It must not live behind PAR5: sprite decoding temporarily maps PAR5 away from game-state page $34, and Vbord may interrupt that decoder. The IRQ does not read per-buffer actor or damage metadata.

The existing A-side player/enemy save-under storage remains usable. Phase 3 allocates 256-byte metadata/damage blocks at $A900 and $AA00, a B-side 128-byte player restoration buffer at $AB00, and four B-side enemy restoration buffers at $AB80-$AD7F. PAR5 remains physical page $34 except while a precompiled sprite stream is being read.

Direct-page pointer $00A2-$00A3 selects the player restoration buffer owned by the current BACK ID. Resident save/restore and bank-3 player composition dereference that pointer. The cold initializer still clones A restoration state into B once, but normal frame transactions never exchange the two 128-byte buffers.

Always-mapped handshake state occupies direct page $008F-$009A: FRONT/BACK IDs, render-pending, commit and simulation sequences, missed-commit count, render-active, write-to-front fault, and initialization state. Cold boot entry $081B copies all 32 KiB of A pages $30-$33 into B pages $2C-$2F while output is blanked, then clones restoration bytes and actor metadata. B is a persistent owner, the compatibility compositor surface occupies physical pages $28-$2B, BACK preparation hydrates actor restoration state from the selected buffer's metadata, and only BACK is mapped through PAR1-PAR4. Setting the bank-3 build constant PERSISTENT_FB to zero retains the phase-3 fixed-A/B-as-scratch fallback for equivalence tests.

Frame transaction

  1. Wait for ownership. Main code does not begin another transaction while FB_RENDER_PENDING is set. After the IRQ commits, map the new back buffer into PAR1-PAR4 and assert that it is not FRONT.
  2. Advance one logical epoch without drawing. Sample input and update movement, collision, timers, score, gate, entity, and death state exactly once. Freeze that state until this image commits. Use FB_SIM_SEQ, not the free-running Vbord counter, for even/odd player-enemy staggering and gameplay timers.
  3. Select actor closure. The implemented bounded policy selects all five moving actors: player plus four enemies. This is a conservative transitive closure over every buffer-old and logical-new rectangle, including actors touched by pending background damage.
  4. Expose clean background. Restore the player first, then every buffer-old enemy from this buffer's own save-under state before applying background damage. No actor is saved or drawn during this phase; player-first removal prevents its save-under from reintroducing an enemy above the clean enemy restore.
  5. Project pending background state. Apply damage in fixed order: maze cells, gates, entities/perimeter, HUD/lives, nest, then popup/death presentation. Clear only the target buffer's pending records after their projections finish.
  6. Maintain clean destinations. Each buffer owns one packed ring byte per enemy: high nibble is the 16-row phase and low three bits are the eight-column phase. An unchanged destination retains its clean save-under. A one-byte horizontal move advances or retreats the column phase and captures one 16-byte exposed column; a two-row vertical move rotates the row phase and captures two eight-byte rows. New, discontinuous, or background-damaged actors normalize to phase zero with a full 128-byte capture.
  7. Draw final layers. Draw roaming enemies, then the player or its presentation replacement last, preserving the current player-over-enemy ownership. Record valid/hidden state, frame key, logical destination, and ring phase for this buffer.
  8. Publish readiness. Restore PAR5 to page $34 and finish target metadata, then briefly mask IRQ, clear FB_RENDER_ACTIVE, and set FB_RENDER_PENDING as one handoff. Do not alter $FF9D or FRONT/BACK IDs in main code.
  9. Commit at Vbord. The IRQ acknowledges $FF92, updates the raw Vbord counter, and, only when pending, derives $C0 or $B0 from BACK, writes $FF9D, exchanges FRONT/BACK IDs, clears pending, and increments both commit and simulation sequences.

If Vbord arrives while rendering is active and readiness is clear, the IRQ leaves the old front selected and increments FB_MISSED_COMMIT. Main finishes the same frozen logical epoch, marks it ready, and waits for a later Vbord. It does not catch up timers, run another logic epoch, change a transient presentation, or switch the target buffer. This prevents partial display and makes overload degrade by slowing simulation rather than skipping retained states. Acceptance still requires zero misses during steady gameplay.

Damage ledger

Damage represents current logical state, not a replay of historical drawing commands. Persistent mutations set the relevant bit for both buffers; applying one buffer clears only its copy. Multiple mutations before a buffer is reused coalesce naturally to the latest state.

The implemented first projection pass uses the stronger scheduler invariant that exactly one committed epoch separates consecutive uses of the same buffer. Bytes 160-177 of each metadata block hold one persistent-intent record: primary/secondary flags, gate, perimeter, letter, and zone payloads, plus the exact dot cell. Before rendering BACK, bank 3 queues the current record for the other buffer, loads and clears BACK's record, projects it from the current frozen logical state, restores the current intent block, and then renders the current epoch. Player, popup, death, and roaming-actor intents are deliberately excluded; their underlay intersections remain inputs to the actor-closure phase. Damage replay enters a background-only pass: gate diagonals/final gates call resident background primitives directly and cannot restore, save, or draw the player.

A damage record must retain enough erase coverage even after logical state changes. Maze damage is a per-buffer cell bitset. Entity removal marks every cell in the old footprint for both buffers before clearing the record; appearance or movement also marks its new footprint. HUD fields use field bits, and gate, perimeter, nest, and presentation records identify the affected region or a full-region rebuild. A coarse class bit without old-footprint coverage is insufficient.

A transient gate diagonal is transaction-scoped state and remains frozen until the diagonal image commits, even after a missed Vbord. Only the following committed epoch advances to the persistent final gate state and marks both buffers, so the diagonal cannot be skipped or reappear. Popup, death, respawn, nest-to-roaming handoff, and actor disappearance use the same visible/hidden plus epoch rule. Stage rebuilds use a full-rebuild barrier: gameplay state is frozen, the front remains displayed, one back image is built and committed, then the other buffer is rebuilt to the same state before normal epochs resume.

Sparse sprite streams

Removing shadow transfers alone leaves approximately 41,730 cycles in the measured frame, so persistent buffering must be paired with a faster native draw representation. The build precompiles each native frame into shared destination-delta command streams: 

delta, opaque_length, pixels...      direct stores
delta, partial_length|$80,
       mask,pixel pairs...           destination-preserving merges
$FF, fb_delta_u16, stage_delta_u8   extended destination
$FF, $0000                          end frame

The normal byte is the framebuffer delta from the preceding command end. Values 152-167 represent an adjacent framebuffer row and map to compact-stage deltas 0-15; skipped rows use the extended form. Independent verification decodes both destinations for every frame. The 128 enemy payload is 22,683 bytes, including its 384-byte index and 296 padding bytes. The sixteen-player payload is 2,294 bytes including its 48-byte index. These payloads replace the 8,192-byte packed enemy atlas and resident packed player frames, fit the free space across GMC banks 2 and 3 after the reserved 4 KiB enemy module, and remove frame-time expansion/cache misses.

During boot, the enemy/player indexes remain at the start of their loaded sparse pages, $35:$A000 and $39:$A000. A draw maps the corresponding index page, resolves the entry, then PAR5 maps only the selected stream page. The decoder may use only registers, direct page, stack, and the PAR1-PAR4 back framebuffer until PAR5 is restored to page $34. All decoder scratch and every IRQ-visible byte must therefore be in physical page $38; per-buffer metadata is read before mapping and written after restoration. PAR5 ownership is scoped and non-nestable.

Aggregate GMC capacity is not a sufficient loader proof because bank 3's 4 KiB enemy module splits its free space. The stream generator must emit an explicit ROM-segment-to-physical-page copy table, avoid the two bank signature words, prohibit a frame from crossing an 8 KiB physical page, and validate every source and destination interval for overlap and bounds.

The delivered ten-record packing uses only CPU-readable bank offsets below $3E00. Bank 2 $0020-$3DFF supplies 15,840 bytes, bank 3 $1800-$3DFF supplies 9,728 bytes, and 1,058 bytes come from pre-runtime bank-3 intervals that exclude signature offsets $0010-$0011. Offsets $3E00-$3FFF are not loader capacity because they appear at forced-RAM/I/O addresses $FE00-$FFFF. Bank 0 $0800-$3DFF adds a 13,824-byte overflow source pool after banks 2 and 3. After the current payloads and two-byte low-RAM proof, the cartridge retains 14,731 CPU-readable source bytes. Runtime destinations and mapping remain separately guarded.

Acceptance gates

  • Decode every enemy and player stream and compare all 4bpp pixels with independent expansion of the packed source.
  • Validate the GMC copy table, signature exclusions, physical destinations, and no-cross-page frame rule; compare all copied RAM payloads with generated inputs.
  • Cold initialization produces identical A/B 30,720-byte hashes and valid per-buffer restoration metadata.
  • No framebuffer writer is reachable outside the render transaction or blanked full-rebuild path.
  • Alternating snapshots converge after dots, entity removal, gates, pickups, colour changes, perimeter resets, nest transitions, actor appearance/disappearance, deaths, stage resets, and transitive three-actor overlap.
  • Force Vbord during PAR5 sprite decoding and confirm that IRQ state, mapping restoration, and the stream output remain exact.
  • Force one- and two-Vbord renderer delays during gate diagonal, popup expiry, enemy release, death, and stage rebuild; confirm one logical advance per commit and no skipped or stale presentation.
  • Vbord commits alternate $C0/$B0 without mixed-frame rows; missed-commit and write-to-front diagnostics remain zero.
  • Profile four-enemy movement, player movement, movement plus animation, gate transition, pickup, and death paths. Each steady frame must be below 29,666 cycles and the normal target is at most 27,000.
  • Repeat the swap and scanout proof on physical CoCo 3 hardware in Phase 10.

Presentation-to-gameplay re-entry

Selected 2026-08-14 for BUG-012: cold ABI $081B remains a blanked-boot-only convergence service. Visible gameplay re-entry must not call it because it fixes logical ownership to A/B and copies a fixed physical source/destination after presentation has already established another scanned owner.

The re-entry owner waits until readiness and active-render flags are clear, restores PAR5 to game-state page $34, invalidates both 256-byte actor/damage ledgers, preserves FRONT/BACK and all commit diagnostics, then marks ownership initialized. The existing full-stage intent rebuilds current BACK and queues the same rebuild for the alternate owner. The complete level-start FRONT remains scanned until the finished stage is committed by the ordinary Vbord IRQ.

Verification rule: observe every Vbord across the full rebuild under natural and controlled reversed-owner sequences. Every pre-commit physical FRONT hash must equal the retained level-start image and the first post-commit hash must equal the complete maze. A post-commit-only comparison is insufficient because it cannot detect exposure of intermediate layered composition.

Approved and assignment-ready ownership correction: the resident main loop currently asserts ACTIVE before calling the presentation module, so the selected re-entry idle test cannot distinguish presentation dispatch from gameplay rendering and deadlocks when implemented literally. The revised BUG-012 contract moves that existing assertion to the module's zero-return gameplay branch, immediately before gameplay mutation begins. This adds no state, preserves the Vbord publisher, and makes ACTIVE mean gameplay mutation or BACK rendering rather than presentation dispatch. IRQs that land inside presentation callbacks are consequently excluded from gameplay-render missed-commit accounting; evidence must label this as corrected counter scope, not a rendering optimization.
