Implementation · decision

Video Mode — decision internal

Phase 2 mode-selection analysis. Picks the GIME hi-res mode, framebuffer layout, and pixel/tile/sprite formats.

Decision (2026-05-08): 320 × 192, 16 colours, format C, single buffer.

GIME settings: $FF98 BP=1 GR=1, $FF99 VRES=00 HRES=111 CRES=10. Row stride 160 bytes. Framebuffer 30 720 bytes.

Inputs to the decision

From the locked design (game/overview):

  • Single non-scrolling playfield per stage.
  • HUD on side panels: the authored map places boxed SPECIAL, EXTRA, and x2x3x5 bands on the left, and score/high-score/credit/part plus lives on the right.
  • Three-colour cycle (blue / yellow / red) drives the letter / heart / vegetable subsystem.
  • ~24×24 tile maze area is the natural scale for the playfield.
  • 8-direction joystick read; constant player speed.

From the platform (GIME, timing):

  • Frame budget at 1.78 MHz, 60 Hz: ≈ 29 666 cycles. Roadmap baseline render allocation: ≈ 18 000 cycles.
  • 16 palette registers, 6-bit colour codes, freely re-loadable.
  • 16-colour mode = 4 bits/pixel = 2 pixels/byte ("format C").
  • Vertical Offset registers point the GIME at any 8-byte-aligned physical-page address.

Hi-res text mode skipped — no per-pixel control, wrong shape for sprite work.

Why 320 × 192 × 16 over 256 × 192 × 16

  1. HUD layout. 320 − 192 (square maze) = 128 px = 64 px per side panel. Comfortable room for 8-tile-wide score bands, the EXTRA / SPECIAL letter rows, and the vegetable icon. 256 − 192 = 32 px per side: 4 tiles per panel — too tight for the design.
  2. Aspect. 320×192 displays at 4:3 PAR ≈ 1.66:1 effective; 256×192 sits awkwardly between modes when stretched.
  3. Familiarity. 320×192×16 is the canonical CoCo 3 graphics-mode demo target; XRoar, hardware monitors, and most CoCo 3 game ROMs assume it.

The cost over 256-wide is 6 144 extra framebuffer bytes (one 8 K page extra) and ~25% more sprite-blit work per draw.

  • Centre: 192 × 192 px = 24 × 24 tiles at 8 × 8 px. Holds maze, dots, all moving entities.
  • Left panel: 64 × 192 px = 8 × 24 tiles. Horizontal boxed SPECIAL, EXTRA, and x2x3x5 bands.
  • Right panel: 64 × 192 px = 8 × 24 tiles. 1ST/current score, TOP/high score, CREDIT, PART, level/bonus placeholders, and three life icons. The arcade UNIVERSAL text is omitted.

The authoritative MAME capture makes the adaptation arithmetic exact: the complete arcade display is 24×30 tiles, or 192×240 pixels at the retained 8×8 scale. Its maze is rows 2–25, exactly 24×24 tiles or 192×192 pixels; the other six rows are HUD. They cannot fit above and below the maze in this CoCo mode. Relocating them to the two side panels provides 16×24 = 384 tile positions for 144 displaced arcade HUD positions. Seven-character SPECIAL, five-character EXTRA, six-character x2x3x5, score digits, lives, level, and vegetable indicators each fit within an eight-tile panel row.

Tile origin in the framebuffer: byte offset = row * 160 + col * 4 (since one 8-px-wide tile = 4 bytes in 16-colour mode). Both centre and panels are 8-px-aligned.

tiled/coco-screen.tmx is the 40×24 authoring reference. The semantic maze occupies columns 8–31. The visible layers and exact panel placement in this hand-authored TMX are authoritative; the build compiler flattens them without regenerating the layout.

Pixel / tile / sprite format

  • Pixel: 4 bits, packed two-per-byte, hi-nibble = leftmost pixel.
  • Tile: 8 × 8 px = 32 bytes. Stored as 8 rows of 4 bytes. Tile data lives in cartridge ROM.
  • Sprite source: 16 × 16 raw 2bpp pixels from the arcade extraction.
  • Runtime player frame: 128 packed 4bpp pixel bytes. The shared transparent blitter preserves the framebuffer wherever source pen 0 occurs, so no separate preserve mask is stored.
  • Glyph (HUD digit, letter): 8 × 8 = 32 bytes, established by Phase 2.6 and rendered through put_char. A separate 16 × 16 score set remains optional and deferred to HUD work.

Phase 2.6 confirmed the tile-coordinate calculation in live XRoar: put_char renders LADYBUG at character row 1, columns 16–22, using the same packed format as maze tiles.

A 2-byte sprite blit (STD ,X++) writes 4 pixels per ~6 cycles. Full 16 × 16 sprite ≈ 64 STDs ≈ 384 cycles plus address arithmetic — ballpark ~600 cycles per sprite. With the largest expected on-screen entity count (player + 4 bugs + 1 skull + 2-3 letters/vegetables ≈ 9 sprites) the per-frame sprite cost is ~5 400 cycles — fits the 18 000-cycle render budget with room for HUD updates.

Phase 4 player renderer — corrected 2026-07-18

  • Arcade sprite index 0, attribute 0, is the canonical Lady Bug player/life source. Sprite 3 is its rotated directional form, not a distinct animation frame.
  • Sprite 0 faces down in raw ROM coordinates. The build rotates it 180 degrees for north, then clockwise for east, south, and west, producing four unique 128-byte packed-pixel records.
  • The single player owns one 128-byte background buffer at $A300. Each update restores the old rectangle, moves, applies dirty-tile changes, saves the new rectangle, then blends the sprite.
  • The opaque sprite anchor is seven pixels left and seven pixels above the semantic cell origin. The byte-aligned save rectangle starts eight pixels left; generated pixels shift right by one inside it. Exhaustive checking found no opaque sprite pixel over a clean maze-wall pixel at any legal two-pixel substep.
  • Movement is two pixels per update, retaining byte and scanline-pair alignment for the fixed 128-byte save rectangle.
  • Movement updates every second Vbord, yielding the MAME-measured 60 pixels/second. Four updates cross one semantic cell. A directional request remains buffered until a cell centre permits the turn; neutral input stops movement immediately after the first manual direction. The arcade startup movement remains automatic until then.

This validates single-buffer save/restore for one entity. Phase 6 must measure multi-entity cost before duplicating the same ownership model for every enemy.

Framebuffer placement

30 720 bytes = 3.75 × 8 KB MMU pages. Will occupy 4 contiguous physical pages, mapped where the rest of the memory layout (Phase 2 task) decides. Vertical-Offset registers $FF9D/$FF9E will point to the start of the first page. The framebuffer does not have to be MMU-mapped to be visible; the GIME reads it via expanded address.

XRoar palette colours — empirical (composite NTSC)

Things deferred to Phase 2 implementation

  1. Exact $FF98 value. BP=1 (graphics), other bits TBD when we decide blink, mono, PAL, lines-per-row. Default LPR=001 / PAL=0 / MOCH=0 likely fine.
  2. Exact $FF99 value. VRES=00 HRES=111 CRES=10 = %00011110 = $1E is the candidate; double-check against coco3-asm-tepolt §Hi-res graphics Table 4-10 once the code is being written.
  3. Memory map. Where in physical RAM does the framebuffer live? See memory-map.
  4. Palette load. 16 entries × 6-bit codes. Initial palette (greens for the maze, primary RGB for the cycle, white/grey for HUD) gets a small data table.
  5. Border colour. Resolved to canonical Black ($FF9A=$00) outside the active framebuffer.
  6. CRES=11 blanking trick. Tepolt notes CRES=11 blanks the screen — useful for setting up the framebuffer without showing tearing. Use it during init.
  7. Single-buffer vs double-buffer. Phase 4 implements single-buffer save/restore. User playtesting must still check visible tearing before this becomes the multi-entity Phase 6 policy.

Risks

  • Frame-budget margin is thin. 5 400 cycles (sprites) + dirty HUD updates + AI + input + sound ≈ 17 000 cycles + IRQ overhead ≈ 17 100. Anything sloppy in the renderer eats the whole margin.
  • Sprite save-restore footprint. Saving the background under each 16×16 sprite costs another 128 bytes copied per sprite; with 9 sprites that's 1 152 bytes/frame just for save, doubled if restore happens too. We may end up wanting a tile-aligned-blit fast path that re-blits the affected tiles from the static maze rather than per-sprite save-restore.
  • The stale Tepolt-source claim (line 84) about HRES=4 byte counts contradicts the same file's mode-table at line 54. Fix the source page.

ROM-budget update — 2026-05-08

Cart was briefly retargeted to 32 K mid-day, then reverted to 16 K when XRoar's 32 K-cart handling came up unverified — see platform/cartridge §"Cart size — 16 K (current)". At 16 K the sprite-storage path matters: pre-converted 4bpp sprites likely overflow; 2bpp+attr packing or curated sprite count is back as the default, with 4bpp pre-conversion only if a measurement at Phase 4 shows it fits.
