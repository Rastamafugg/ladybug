Lady Bug — Game Design Document internal

Design specification for the CoCo 3 native-hardware port of the 1981 Universal arcade game Lady Bug. Goal: arcade fidelity wherever the platform allows; documented adaptations where it doesn't.

High-level

A maze-chase game in the Pac-Man lineage with two distinguishing mechanics: rotating turnstile gates that the player (but not enemies) can shift to redirect chasers, and collectible letters that spell EXTRA (extra life) and SPECIAL (bonus / extra credit). The player is a ladybug; enemies are a rotating cast of insects.

The arcade ran on a Z80 @ 4 MHz with two TI SN76489 sound chips at 240×192 @ 60.11 Hz vertical-orientation raster.

Aspect-ratio adaptation (CoCo 3)

Arcade: 240 wide × 192 tall (vertical monitor). Maze is a single non-scrolling screen; HUD/score graphics sit above and below the playfield.

CoCo 3 target: horizontal display (e.g. 320×192 hi-res). The playfield itself is square-ish and fits as-is, but the HUD bands the arcade puts above/below the maze are rotated to side panels:

  • Left panel: boxed SPECIAL, EXTRA, and x2x3x5 bands. Letters begin Grey and illuminate Red, Yellow, or Blue respectively when a matching-colour item advances that target.
  • Right panel: Light Green 1ST/score, Red TOP/high score, White CREDIT, Blue PART, Green vegetable/bonus value, and three player-palette life icons. UNIVERSAL is omitted.

Playfield

  • Single non-scrolling screen per stage.
  • Maze is a fixed grid of corridors with a central nest where enemies spawn.
  • 20 green turnstile gates placed at corridor junctions. The ladybug walking through one rotates it 90°; this can open or seal off paths. Enemies cannot turn gates.
    • Constraint: the maze design guarantees that no gate configuration can fully isolate any region of the maze.
  • Border circuit timer: the outer maze boxes begin White with Black separation. Starting at the thirteenth top box from the left, directly below the arcade EXTRA T, one box turns Green every nine 60 Hz frames on part 1, every six frames on parts 2â€“4, and every three frames from part 5 onward. After all 92 boxes are Green, a progressive White sweep follows at the same cadence. These thresholds come from the arcade timer routine at Z80 $35E3.
  • Maze colours: regular walls and the two-pixel inner perimeter are Pink; turnstile bars are Green and their pivots Purple. Flowers have a White centre with four Light Blue corner pixels on Black.

Entities

Player (ladybug)

  • 3 reserve lives at start. The HUD counter excludes the currently active ladybug. Consuming the final visible reserve still starts one replacement; game over occurs only when that replacement dies with the reserve counter already at zero.
  • Bonus life at 30,000 points (one-time award).
  • Constant movement speed across all levels (only enemies get faster).
  • 4-direction joystick control (no diagonal).
  • Arcade ROM control state is now identified: current direction at $6198 uses Left/Down/Right/Up bits $01/$02/$04/$08; the requested direction occupies the high nibble of $6026; and player coordinates are $6027/$6028. The control routine at $35FF-$388B accepts exactly one active direction bit and ignores neutral or multi-direction combinations.
  • Arcade collision uses a packed, immutable passage-mask table at ROM $0DA2; a request is legal when the destination cell mask contains its direction bit. Row/column alignment masks at $0DE4/$0DFA select nearby corridor centres. Accepted perpendicular turns move one pixel on the new axis while correcting the old axis one pixel per frame toward $6196/$6197.
  • A perpendicular request remains eligible throughout the three partial two-pixel steps after an aligned junction. If that passage is legal, the arcade corrects the old-axis offset diagonally while beginning the new direction; at the next eight-pixel cell the window has expired. The same rule applies when a legal push rotates a gate.
  • Touching an enemy costs one life. Enemy reset/position behavior after player death must be measured from MAME.
  • Touching a skull is instant death.

Enemies (insects)

  • Eight distinct insect types. Levels 1–8 each introduce a new type (one type per level on those stages).
  • From level 9 onward each stage features four different insect types simultaneously.
  • AI: chase the ladybug through corridors; cannot shift gates.
  • Current CoCo implementation boundary: released enemies exit north, roam continuously with pseudo-random legal non-reversing junction choices, traverse passages parallel to a gate's current bar without rotating it, and do not re-enter the den. Detection range and pursuit remain pending.
  • Killable by luring into a skull.
  • A skull-killed enemy returns to the central den, waits briefly, and is released again.
  • Frozen for several seconds when the player eats a vegetable; still lethal on contact during the freeze.
  • StrategyWiki claim to verify: enemies wander semi-randomly until the player is close, then pursue steadily; entering the den is safe while enemies remain inside it.
  • Speed increases per level; player speed does not.

Skulls

  • Randomly placed each stage and stationary.
  • Lethal to both ladybug and enemies on contact.
  • MAME directly confirms two visible skulls on stage 1. Until later stages are measured, use the FAQ table: 2 on stage 1; 3 on stages 2–4; 4 on stages 5–8; 5 on stages 9–17; 6 on stage 18 onward.
  • Player contact removes every skull before the death animation. Enemy contact removes only the touched skull and returns that enemy to the den population. The CoCo implementation preserves collected flowers, bonuses, gates, score, and stage state during the new-ladybug entrance.

Hearts and letters — separate entities, global colour cycle

Hearts and letters are distinct items placed at distinct positions in the maze.

Global colour cycle. All on-stage letters and all on-stage hearts share a single colour state that changes simultaneously and instantaneously across every item. The colour cycles red → yellow → blue → red. MAME 0.220 measurement at 60 Hz gives exactly 30 frames Red, 150 frames Yellow, and 420 frames Blue after the initial partial cycle: 0.5 s, 2.5 s, and 7 s. The StrategyWiki estimate of roughly four seconds Yellow is not supported by the installed ROM.

Compact frame anchors and measurement limits are retained in gameplay_rules_reference.json.

Three targets compete for collected items: the EXTRA word, the SPECIAL word, and the heart multiplier. Each target has a fixed colour: SPECIAL is Red, EXTRA is Yellow, and the heart multiplier is Blue. Because the global cycle has every item displaying the same colour at any moment, and each target is bound to a different colour, no tie can occur: at any instant at most one target is "active."

Letters (word-spelling items: E, X, T, R, A, S, P, C, I, L — E and A appear in both words)

  • HUD target letters begin Grey and change to the target's fixed colour only when the matching letter is collected while the item is that colour.
  • Stage-one MAME capture directly shows six synchronized bonus circles. The FAQ specifies three hearts plus one letter from X/T/R, one from S/P/C/I/L, and one from E/A, all replacing randomized food positions.
  • A letter advances a word only when collected while the current global colour equals that word's assigned colour.
  • E and A: collected at the EXTRA-colour → advance EXTRA; collected at the SPECIAL-colour → advance SPECIAL; collected at the multiplier-colour → no word progress.
  • Letters specific to one word collected at any colour other than their word's colour → no progress.
  • Off-target collection still scores the colour's base value (100/300/800).

Hearts (multiplier items)

  • The Grey x2, x3, and x5 HUD fields illuminate Blue in that order as matching Blue hearts are collected.
  • A heart only counts toward the multiplier when collected at the multiplier-colour. Counted hearts accumulate in-stage:
    • 1st → ×2 multiplier on subsequent dots/letters.
    • 2nd → ×3.
    • 3rd → ×5.
  • Off-colour hearts score the base colour value but do not progress the multiplier.
  • Pickup score graphics replace the player for exactly 30 frames, independent of input. An active multiplier appears simultaneously in White beneath the score but inside the same collectible footprint. MAME measured the multiplier top-left at (+7,+10) from the 16×16 score-sprite origin. The glyphs are arcade character-sheet cells R5C6 (x2), R4C6 (x3), and R2C6 (x5).
  • Multiplier does not apply to the centre vegetable.
  • Multiplier resets at stage clear.

Word completion rewards

  • EXTRA (5 letters E-X-T-R-A) → +1 life; the target resets.
  • SPECIAL (7 letters S-P-E-C-I-A-L) → 10,000 points in the CoCo v1 adaptation; the target resets. StrategyWiki describes the arcade reward as a free game.
  • StrategyWiki claim to verify: completing either word ends the current stage immediately.

Vegetable

  • Appears in the central nest area once the fourth enemy has been released.
  • Eating it: bonus points (table above) + freezes all enemies for several seconds.
  • Implementation decision, corrected 2026-07-27: the trigger is current active-enemy count, not cumulative releases. The vegetable never contributes to normal stage completion. After collection, the nest remains empty while four enemies are active; a skull death below four restores the dormant den actor, and a stage/death reset reinitializes the nest. Freeze duration is 300 Vbords (5 seconds) pending MAME measurement.
  • Frozen enemies remain lethal. StrategyWiki says they resume with a speed increase when the vegetable tune ends; verify duration and speed change in MAME.
  • If a skull-killed enemy returns to the den, StrategyWiki says the vegetable can reappear after that enemy is released again; verify the exact recurrence condition.

Stage flow

  1. Maze drawn (single fixed layout, identical every stage). All 117 CoCo placement candidates begin as flowers; six bonuses and the stage-dependent skull count then replace unique randomized candidates. Only the three centre-bottom entrance cells remain permanently empty.
  2. Border timer starts; Lady Bug walks north through the three-cell entrance, stops at cell (12,18), and remains stationary until directional input. Enemies leave the den at completed border circuits and roam the maze.
  3. Stage-one MAME advances one of the 92 border boxes every 9 frames. A complete circuit is 828 frames (13.8 s). The following circuit progressively changes the Green boxes back to White at the same cadence; it does not clear every box in one frame.
  4. The vegetable appears after all four enemies have left the den. Arcade observation is still required to determine its availability duration, recurrence after an enemy is killed, and level-dependent timing.
  5. Normal stage clear requires every flower plus all six bonus circles; word completion also requests an immediate stage advance. Controlled arcade collection remains the final precedence check.
  6. Death (enemy contact or skull) → the directed sheet sequence runs from R6C7 down through R0C7, then R5C10 down through R0C10. The latter cells are ROM codes 82–87, not the previously recorded 64–68. Final angel R7C11 is ROM code 88: it holds 30 frames, then follows the measured stepped right/centre/left/centre upward path. The rightmost active TMX reserve marker then becomes the animated player sprite and walks 24 scanlines off-screen; only then is the reserve count decremented and the replacement enters. If the reserve count was already zero at death, the current implementation enters an inert terminal hold pending the Phase 9 game-over screen.

The maze is a single fixed layout used for every stage. Per-stage variation comes from enemy types/count/speed, the vegetable, randomized skull and bonus positions, and the three randomized letter selections. Level one mixes power-on RAM and joystick state into the placement LFSR; later levels continue from the per-frame-advanced state.

The authored Phase 9 presentation maps follow measured arcade states: a centered Lady Bug title with coin prompts, instructions, a nine-row high-score table with Universal branding, a qualifying-score name-entry grid, and a level-start panel showing part number, vegetable value/name, skull count, enemy lineup, and GOOD LUCK. The instructions, name-entry, and level-start centres are combined with the existing CoCo side HUD.

Audio

Arcade has two SN76489 chips. CoCo 3 has a single 6-bit DAC. Significant adaptation needed — sound design is its own page (TBD). See platform/sound.

Input

  • Joystick (4-direction). Arcade uses a single 4-way stick.
  • Optional keyboard fallback (arrow keys) — to confirm with user.

Decisions locked (2026-05-05)

  • HUD: the hand-authored tiled/coco-screen.tmx is authoritative. Its left panel has horizontal boxed SPECIAL, EXTRA, and x2x3x5 bands; its right panel has score/high-score/credit/part placeholders and the life icons. UNIVERSAL is omitted.
  • Hearts and letters are separate maze entities. Global colour cycle; SPECIAL is Red, EXTRA is Yellow, and the heart multiplier is Blue.
  • SPECIAL reward: 10,000 points. No bonus round in v1.
  • 3 starting lives, one-time bonus life at 30,000 points.
  • Single fixed maze layout used for every stage.
  • The former fixed-skull-count assumption is superseded. Use the provisional two-to-six stage progression from StrategyWiki until measured from MAME.
  • No "Vegetable Harvest" bonus round in the first release.

Deferred (not blocking initial implementation)

  1. Colour-cycle reset — stage-one durations are measured; determine whether stage-clear or death resets the cycle.
  2. Later-stage border/vegetable timing — stage one is 9 frames per box; measure stages 2 and 5, the vegetable appearance point, availability duration, and relationship to the next enemy release.
  3. Enemy and skull behavior — verify wander-to-pursuit switching, den safety, skull progression, skull removal after player death, enemy den return/re-release, vegetable recurrence, and post-freeze speed increase.
  4. Stage completion — verify whether all six bonus circles are mandatory and whether either completed word immediately advances the stage.
  5. Two-player scope — StrategyWiki documents separate one- and two-player start buttons; decide whether alternating two-player mode is required for CoCo v1.
  6. Sound design — DAC waveform tables, jingles.
  7. Input fallback — joystick is primary; keyboard arrow-key fallback to confirm.
